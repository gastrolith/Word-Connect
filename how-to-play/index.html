How to Play

The objective is to build a bridge between two starting words. You do this by submitting words. When you submit a word that is semantically related to either word immediately to the left or right of the submission box, the word is added to the current word chain. This continues until the submitted word is related to both words immediately adjacent to the submission box, in which case you have bridged the beginning words and the game is over. Two words being semantically related means that there is significant overlap in their meaning, or that there is a lexical relationship between them. Every possible word in the game has at least five words connected to it that are semantically related. A word can contain hyphens and spaces, and can also be a proper noun. The difficulty determines the minimum number of words required to connect the starter words, with higher difficulties requiring longer word chains.

How does it work?

A graph (i.e., a set of nodes, the words, and edges, a connection between two words that indicates that they are semantically related) governs the relationships between words in this game. The graph was built using word2vec vectors, and the cosine similarities between them, a proxy for semantic relatedness. Words whose vectors have high cosine similarity tend to be related in meaning, while low cosine similarity tends to suggest little overlap in meaning (e.g., ). Every word shares an edge with the five other words that they have the their highest cosine similarity with, as well as any word with a cosine similarity higher than a certain emperically-determined threshold. Essentially, through submitting words, you are attempting to find a path between two nodes.
